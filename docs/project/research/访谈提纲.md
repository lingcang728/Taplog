# Interview Guide (Stage 2.3)

- **Interviewers:** Huang Yitao leads; every member runs at least one interview.
- **Participants:** 3–5 people, done by 12/1. Each interview takes 10–15 minutes.
- **Format:** online, either a WeChat voice call or written answers using the text version in Section 3.
- **Language:** questions are in English. Participants may answer in Chinese; record their words as they said them and translate when writing them up. A Chinese translation of the questions is in Section 6.
- This guide also goes into the report Appendix as it is.

---

## 1. Who to interview

**Inclusion criteria**
- Watches recordings or replays of technical talks, conference talks or research talks at least a few times per semester.
- Studies or works in software development, e.g. computer science students or developers.
- Is not a member of our group and has not heard the details of our project.

**Aim for a mix**
- at least one careful viewer who takes notes;
- at least one person who mainly reads AI summaries and rarely watches the full video;
- at least one person who does not trust AI summaries.

---

## 2. Interview script

### Opening (about 1 minute, read aloud)

> We are working on an interaction design project and would like to learn how people use **recordings of technical talks**. It takes about 10 minutes. There are no right or wrong answers; please just tell us about your real experience.
> May I record this conversation (or keep the written answers)? It is only used for our notes, and your name will not appear in our report.

Start recording only after the participant agrees. If they say no, take written notes only.

**Do not describe our design idea at the start,** and do not use words like "late caveat" or "traceable". Otherwise the participant will tend to agree with us.

### Main questions

Ask them in this order. Use the follow-up prompts only as needed.

**Q1. The last time**
> When was the last time you went back to a recording of a technical talk, or to its AI summary? What was the talk, and why did you go back to it?

- Follow-up: Was it your first time watching it, or were you going back to find something?
- Follow-up: Why did you go back instead of relying on what you remembered?
- What we want to learn: in what situations people go back, e.g. before applying a conclusion, quoting it in their own writing, or checking before a discussion.

**Q2. How you searched**
> What were you looking for, and how exactly did you look for it? Did you find it? Roughly how long did it take?

- Follow-up: Did you drag the progress bar, search the subtitles, use timestamps in an AI summary, or something else?
- Follow-up: Which step was the most annoying? Was there a time you searched for a long time and gave up?
- Follow-up: Once you found the spot, did you listen to just that sentence, or did you also listen to what came before and after?
- What we want to learn: current search strategies, how long they take, and where people get stuck.

**Q3. Trust in AI notes**
> Have you used AI-generated summaries or notes for talks, for example Feishu Minutes, Tongyi Tingwu or Bilibili's AI summary? When do you trust the notes directly, and when do you go back to the audio to check?

- Follow-up: When you go back to the audio, how much do you usually listen to? At what point do you feel "OK, confirmed"?
- Follow-up: Have you ever found that an AI summary was wrong or left something out?
- For people who have never used AI summaries, ask instead: When do you go back to the original video to check your own notes?
- What we want to learn: how people check, and especially whether they stop after one sentence or keep listening.

**Q4. Things added later** (always ask this one last)
> Have you ever thought you understood a speaker's conclusion, and only later found that the speaker added a condition, a limitation or a correction somewhere else in the same talk?

- If yes:
  - How did you find out?
  - Before you found out, had you already used your first understanding?
  - What trouble did it cause?
- If no: Do you think this could happen? If it did, do you think you would notice?
- What we want to learn: whether this problem shows up in real experience, and how people discover it. "No, never" is also a valid answer; record it as it is.

### Closing (about 1 minute)

> Is there anything else about watching recorded talks or using AI summaries that you find inconvenient or annoying?

Thank them. If they seem willing, ask: "When we have a prototype, could we ask you to try it for about 15 minutes?" Note down who says yes; we can contact them for the user test.

### Tips for the interviewer

- **Ask about one specific time.** If they say "I usually…", follow up with "What happened the most recent time?"
- **Do not put words in their mouth.** Ask "How did that feel?", not "Was that annoying?"
- **Do not explain or defend.** If they say existing tools are good enough, write it down and do not argue.
- **Ask "Why?" and "And then?"** The useful part is often in the second answer.
- **Write down specifics:** tool names, which talk, and any numbers they mention.

## 3. Text version (when the participant answers in writing)

> Hi! We are working on an interaction design project and would like to learn how people use recordings of technical talks. Please answer the four questions below based on your **most recent real experience**. A few sentences each is enough, and you may answer in Chinese. Your answers are only used for our notes, and your name will not appear in our report. Thank you!
>
> 1. When was the last time you went back to a recording of a technical talk, or to its AI summary? What was the talk, and why did you go back?
> 2. What were you looking for, and how exactly did you look for it (dragging the progress bar, searching subtitles, using timestamps in an AI summary, …)? Did you find it? Roughly how long did it take? Which step was the most annoying?
> 3. Have you used AI-generated summaries of talks (Feishu Minutes, Tongyi Tingwu, Bilibili AI summary, …)? When do you trust them directly, and when do you go back to the audio to check? How much do you usually listen to when you check?
> 4. Have you ever thought you understood a speaker's conclusion, and only later found that the speaker added a condition, a limitation or a correction somewhere else in the same talk? How did you find out? If this has never happened to you, just say so.

Written answers tend to be short. Pick one or two points to follow up on, e.g. "You said it took a long time — roughly how long? How did you find it in the end?"

## 4. Note sheet (one per interview)

Save each interview as `P1.md`, `P2.md`, … in this folder. **Do not write real names.**

```markdown
# P1

- Date: 2026-11-__
- Format: voice / text
- Background: (e.g. CS master's student, year 2; watches 2–3 conference replays a month; often uses Bilibili AI summary)
- Consent to record: yes / no
- Interviewer:

| Quote (as close to verbatim as possible) | Issue it shows | Implication for our design |
|---|---|---|
| "…" | | |
```

- **Quote:** copy what the participant said. You may drop filler words but must not change the meaning. If they answered in Chinese, keep the Chinese and add an English translation.
- **Issue it shows:** one short phrase, e.g. "stops after one sentence", "cannot find the exact spot", "does not trust AI summaries".
- **Implication for our design:** what this means for us. Write "to discuss" if unsure.

---

## 5. After the interviews

1. Write up each interview on the same day. AI may turn a recording or transcript into the three-column table, but the interviewer must check every quote.
2. Finish all interviews by 12/1. Put all the "Issue it shows" entries together and mark the issues that appear two or more times.
3. At the 12/2 meeting, decide: did anyone actually experience "a condition added later that I missed"? Even one real case is strong evidence. If nobody did, report that honestly and judge it together with the online reviews and the product walkthrough.

## 6. Chinese translation of the questions (for participants who prefer Chinese)

1. 你最近一次回头去看一场技术讲座或演讲的录像（或者它的 AI 总结），是什么时候？看的是什么？为什么要回去看？
2. 当时你想找什么？具体怎么找的（拖进度条、搜字幕、看 AI 总结里的时间点……）？找到了吗？大概花了多久？哪一步最麻烦？
3. 你用过 AI 生成的讲座总结吗（飞书妙记、通义听悟、B 站 AI 总结等）？什么情况下直接相信，什么情况下会回去听原声核对？回去听的时候一般听多长？
4. 有没有遇到过：以为懂了讲者的某个结论，后来才发现他在讲座别的地方又补充了条件、讲了限制，或者改了说法？当时怎么发现的？没遇到过也请直接说。
